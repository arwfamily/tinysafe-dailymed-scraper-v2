Finding · DailyMed labels

Same ingredient list, different label

By Angela Lee, Founder · ARW House · Updated October 8, 2026

55of 340

Each door is one baby or kids formula. Filled: same ingredients as a label that does not say baby or kids.

55 of the 340 baby and kids sunscreen formulas listed in the FDA's DailyMed label database list the same ingredients in their Drug Facts as a sunscreen whose label does not say baby or kids: the same active ingredients at the same percentages and the same inactive ingredients in the same order. In at least 45 cases both labels name the same labeler.

Registry dataReview fileMethod

Not a violation: nothing requires a baby or kids sunscreen to have a different formula. The finding compares what the label records state; it does not show how the products are made.

DailyMed lists drug labels submitted to the FDA, including labels for products made in US facilities for other markets. A listing is not proof that a product is on US shelves today.

What this means

The US sunscreen monograph sets one limit per active ingredient. It has no separate formula rules for products labeled for babies or kids. FDA M020

So a baby or kids label can carry exactly the same formula as another sunscreen. This finding counts how often the Drug Facts of a baby or kids label, as filed with the FDA, match another label item for item.

How we counted

Population: unique formulations of sunscreen labels that say baby or kids (FDA DailyMed; selected by rule from the label, with edge cases decided by hand and every decision recorded with its reason; labels for another market, labels that do not meet US sunscreen limits as listed, and multi-product listings left out). That is 340 unique formulations, of which 175 use only mineral actives (zinc oxide and/or titanium dioxide). DailyMed snapshot 2026-10-08.

Questions

Is it wrong to sell the same formula under a baby label?

Not a violation: nothing requires a baby or kids sunscreen to have a different formula. The finding compares what the label records state; it does not show how the products are made.

How was "the same" decided?

Both labels list the same active ingredients at the same percentages, and their Drug Facts, as filed with the FDA, list the same inactive ingredients in the same order. Pairs whose SPF, product form or named actives differ, or whose Drug Facts could not be read, were left out.
