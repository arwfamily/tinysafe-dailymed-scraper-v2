## Source metadata problems

- **fda_voices**: the title is wrong. The actual title is "Shedding **New** Light on Sunscreen Absorption" (Woodcock & Michele, 2019), not "Shedding More Light".
- **fda_m020**: the URL points to the 2021 posting, which still lists PABA and trolamine and has no bemotrizinol. "Amended 2026" does not match the linked file. Link the current consolidated M020.
- **eu_nano_zno**: covers only nano ZnO (Reg. 2016/621). Nano TiO2 needs Reg. 2016/1143 (13 July 2016) as its own source.
- **eu_mi, eu_nano_zno**: the URLs are legislation.gov.uk, the UK-retained copy. Prefer eur-lex.europa.eu for "EU law".
- **aap_uv**: the page credits only "American Academy of Pediatrics"; it does not name the Council on Environmental Health. Drop the council from the title or verify it.
- **cir_salicylates**: superseded by the 2025 Amended Safety Assessment (doi 10.1177/10915818251389456).
- **tga_args**: now outdated for 4-MBC; see the 2026-09 TGA safety review.
- **downs_2021**: the product count is inconsistent between reports (15 vs 17/16). The release describes "sunscreen and anti-aging products".
- **sccs_homosalate**: the fetched document is SCCS/1638/21 (adopted 2 Dec 2021). The fetch did not show "addendum" in it. The earlier opinion is SCCS/1622/20. Check the title wording.
- **hc_tips**: the page's start date is 2018-06-01, but its metadata says issued 2021-10-27. Either is defensible.
- **chemlinked_mocra**: the page shows no article date. The agenda it describes came out 2026-07-03.
- **jama_2020**: OK; "(adults)" is fine (48 healthy participants, mean age 38.7).
- All other metadata checked was correct: dates, orgs, the effective dates of the final orders, EU regulation dates, the OEHHA listing date.

## Missing counterpoints / out-of-date items

1. **mbc_eu_ban**: the Australia counterpoint is stale. The TGA completed a 4-MBC safety review in Sept 2026, and a consultation on controls, including possible prohibition, opened 11 Sep 2026. Update it or drop it.
2. **uv_absorbing_inactive O1**: cite the 2025 CIR amended salicylates report instead of 2003 (after confirming it covers both esters).
3. **octocrylene F4**: label the TGA 383 ppm figure as a recommendation or proposal. I found no final Poisons Standard decision. Separately, ASEAN reportedly bans benzophenone in cosmetics from 19 Nov 2026 (Australian Sunscreen Council post; not verified at the primary source).
4. **homosalate_eu / filters "not known"**: a 2026 *Environmental Science & Technology* study is reported (secondary source yourlifechoices.com.au, 11 Sep 2026; primary not verified) to have measured homosalate metabolites in pooled Australian urine samples (~18,700 people, 2012–2023). About 80% of pooled child samples reportedly exceeded the German HBM-I value and 53% exceeded HBM-II. The TGA reportedly said detection "does not itself indicate a health risk". If confirmed, the filters not_known line "no comparable study in children" should be narrowed to "no controlled plasma-absorption study in children". Biomonitoring data in children (also NHANES urinary oxybenzone) do exist. The TGA July 2025 review also proposed lowering homosalate and oxybenzone limits in Australia; the outcome is pending.
5. **mi_mci**: the topic is MI/MCI but the facts cover only MI. Add: the EU banned the MCI/MI mixture in leave-on products (Reg. (EU) 1003/2014, applicable 16 July 2015) and limits MI to 0.0015% in rinse-off. A balancing counterpoint: CIR (2019 amended) found MI safe in rinse-off products up to 100 ppm, and in leave-on products when formulated to be non-sensitizing, as shown by a QRA. The US has no MI restriction.
6. **parabens**: there is no US counterpoint. Add FDA's position ("Parabens in Cosmetics" page: FDA does not have information showing parabens as used in cosmetics have an effect on human health) and/or the CIR's 2019 conclusion that parabens are safe as used. Also note that the EU allows methyl- and ethylparaben up to 0.4% each (0.8% total parabens in mixtures) and bans five other parabens (Reg. 358/2014).
7. **formaldehyde_releaser**: no counterpoint. Consider CIR conclusions for the common releasers (e.g. DMDM hydantoin, safe as used), plus FDA's planned Nov 2026 NPRM, which covers only formaldehyde and releasers in heated hair-straightening products, not leave-on skin products.
8. **spray**: O1 can mention inhalation only if it cites the 2021 proposed order itself, not the Q&A page.
9. **insect_repellent O1**: this is not an opposing view, since it agrees with using separate products. Move it to facts, or replace it with a real counterpoint if one exists (I did not find an official body endorsing combination products).
10. **hawaii**: still current. Act 104 stands, and no additional Hawaii statewide ingredient ban was enacted (SB132/HB102 stalled). Consider noting that Maui County separately bans sale of non-mineral sunscreens (ordinance effective Oct 2022). That is relevant to chemical-filter products, but I did not verify it in this pass.
11. **octocrylene F1**: for balance, note that the Downs study's lead author heads Haereticus Environmental Laboratory, which petitioned FDA to pull octocrylene products.
